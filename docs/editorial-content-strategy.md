# Staffroom Review — Editorial Content Strategy & Story Standards

## Purpose

This document governs the expansion of homepage placeholders into publishable Staffroom Review stories and establishes the content, headline, article-length, SEO, authorship, newsletter, blog and editorial-hold standards that apply across the site.

The objective is not to copy another publisher's voice. The benchmark is the strongest recurring practices visible across established education publications while preserving Staffroom Review's own human, reflective and editorial identity.

## Benchmark set

The survey focused on established education publishers with strong educator audiences and/or significant education reach:

- Edutopia — practical teaching, first-person educator experience, classroom practice and community-oriented editorial.
- Education Week — broad K–12 coverage, teaching and learning, opinion, Q&A, special reports, profiles and interactive formats.
- Tes Magazine — education news, analysis, research, teaching and learning, classroom practice and multiple newsletter products.
- Chalkbeat — local/national education reporting, Ideas columns, first-person work and tightly targeted newsletters.
- EdSurge — human-centred reporting, narrative essays, commentary, research and educator voices.
- The Hechinger Report — research/data-driven education reporting with classroom and campus reporting.
- The Ken — the closest Indian editorial benchmark for narrative discipline and long-form depth; it is a style/reading-rhythm reference, not a content-model authority for Staffroom Review.

## What the benchmark shows

### 1. Story formats should be explicit

The strongest sites do not treat every story as a generic article. They distinguish formats such as:

- Reported feature
- First-person / personal essay
- Profile
- Interview / Q&A
- Analysis / interpretation
- Practical guide
- Research / evidence piece
- Visual story
- Blog / short-form note
- Newsletter edition

Staffroom Review will use these format labels internally so the editorial team can choose an appropriate structure, length, headline style and page treatment.

### 2. Headlines are descriptive but still editorial

Observed patterns include:

- specific problem + consequence
- a compelling tension or contradiction
- a question that creates a genuine information gap
- a human subject + change/insight
- a practical promise
- a memorable phrase followed by a clarifying clause

Staffroom Review should prefer clear, distinctive headlines over vague literary lines. The headline should still sound like Staffroom Review rather than a search-engine query.

### 3. Deks do important work

A strong dek should answer some combination of:

- What is this story about?
- Why should the reader care?
- What is the tension, surprise, question or promise?
- What perspective will the story add?

Target: normally 20–45 words for standard stories, with flexibility for feature packages.

### 4. Long-form has to earn its length

The Ken explicitly says its longform stories average about 2,500–3,000 words. Its education stories also show 11–16 minute reading times. This is useful evidence that substantial narrative depth can be part of a premium editorial proposition when the reporting and structure justify it.

Staffroom Review will therefore not impose one site-wide article length. Instead, use format-specific editorial bands.

## Staffroom Review editorial length bands

These are planning standards, not SEO requirements.

| Format | Primary use | Target body length |
|---|---|---:|
| Short note / brief | timely observation, announcement, small idea | 350–700 words |
| Blog / Staffroom Note | personal reflection, useful observation, compact argument | 700–1,200 words |
| Practical guide | classroom or teaching practice | 900–1,500 words |
| Interview / Q&A | teacher, leader, researcher, practitioner | 1,200–2,000 words |
| First-person essay | lived experience, reflection, voice | 1,200–2,000 words |
| Profile | person, school, practice, institution | 1,500–2,500 words |
| Reported feature | substantial reported story | 1,800–3,000 words |
| Long Read | signature Staffroom narrative/essay | 2,500–4,000 words |
| Visual story | image-led narrative with concise text | 600–1,500 words plus visual captions |
| Newsletter edition | curated or original editorial letter | 400–900 words |

The editor may exceed these bands when the subject genuinely requires it. Never lengthen a story to hit a number.

Google states explicitly that there is no preferred word count for search ranking; completeness, originality, usefulness, expertise and satisfying the reader are more important. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

## Headline standard

### Article headline

Default target:
- 8–16 words.
- Usually aim to keep the visible headline concise enough to survive mobile display without losing meaning.
- Use the clearest natural phrase for the subject.
- Search intent should be reflected where relevant, but keyword stuffing is prohibited.
- Questions are permitted only when the story genuinely answers them.
- Avoid generic clickbait, exaggerated claims and artificial curiosity.

Google recommends descriptive, distinctive titles and notes that long headlines can be truncated on some devices. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link

### Homepage/card headline

The homepage headline is the same editorial headline unless a deliberate packaging change is approved. Do not silently replace a full title with a weaker teaser merely to fit a card.

For constrained card layouts, line wrapping and visual truncation should be solved in CSS/layout before changing the editorial wording.

### Dek

Default target:
- 20–45 words.
- One clear idea.
- Adds information rather than paraphrasing the headline.
- Can contain the reader-facing benefit, tension or context.

## Story opening standard

The first paragraphs should create immediate relevance without turning every story into the same formula.

Preferred openings:
- a specific scene
- a consequential observation
- a revealing human moment
- a sharply framed problem
- a surprising fact followed by context
- a direct statement of the central question

Avoid:
- generic education clichés
- throat-clearing introductions
- repeating the headline
- SEO-first keyword stuffing
- unsupported sweeping claims

EdSurge explicitly describes its editorial approach as human-centred, narrative and designed to move beyond "what happened" toward why it matters. https://www.edsurge.com/about-edsurge

## Body structure

Default long-form structure:

1. Opening scene / proposition
2. Context
3. Development through examples, evidence, voices or scenes
4. Tension / complication
5. Broader meaning
6. Resolution or open question
7. Related reading / next path

Use subheads when they materially improve scanning. Do not turn narrative features into a sequence of generic SEO headings.

Practical pieces may use:
- H2 sections
- numbered steps
- examples
- pull quotes
- source notes
- related stories

## Evidence and expertise

Where a story makes a factual, research or institutional claim:

- identify the source
- provide enough context for the reader to understand what was measured
- distinguish reporting from interpretation
- attribute contested claims
- link to primary sources where practical
- name the author and relevant credentials when important to trust

Education Week, Hechinger and EdSurge routinely pair stories with named authors, expert voices, research/data and context; this is a major part of their authority proposition. https://hechingerreport.org/sponsorship/ https://www.edsurge.com/about-edsurge https://www.edweek.org/about/

## Author system

Every publishable story should have:

- author name
- author role/description
- author page URL
- publication date
- updated date only when meaningful
- optional reading time
- content format
- primary topic/section
- image credit where applicable

Google recommends author name and author URL in Article structured data, and recommends publishing dates plus datePublished / dateModified when applicable. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates

## SEO standard

SEO is part of editorial production, not a separate rewrite after publication.

Every article should have:

- unique page title
- unique H1 matching or closely corresponding to the editorial headline
- unique meta description
- canonical URL
- descriptive slug
- primary topic/category
- meaningful internal links
- descriptive anchor text
- descriptive image alt text when the image communicates information
- image credit
- Article/BlogPosting structured data as appropriate
- author and date data
- Open Graph/Twitter social metadata
- sitemap inclusion
- breadcrumb data when the page hierarchy warrants it

Google recommends people-first content, descriptive titles/headings, crawlable links and descriptive link/alt text. Article structured data can help Google understand article title, author, image and dates. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article

### Search-intent rule

Do not create a separate story simply because a keyword exists.

Each SEO-oriented story must pass this test:
- Is this a question Staffroom Review would genuinely publish for its audience?
- Do we have first-hand expertise, reporting, evidence or a distinctive point of view?
- Does the story provide enough value that the reader would not need to immediately search elsewhere?

This follows Google's current people-first guidance. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

## Internal-linking standard

Each published story should link naturally to:
- at least 2 relevant Staffroom stories when available
- its parent topic page
- a related format/series when applicable

Every important article page should also be reachable from another crawlable page.

Google recommends descriptive, crawlable links and says every page the site cares about should be linked from at least one other findable page. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/get-started-developers

## Image/editorial presentation standard

The image is part of the story proposition.

Prefer:
- original photography
- commissioned illustration
- licensed editorial photography
- documentary/observational images
- visual evidence relevant to the story

Avoid generic "teacher at laptop" imagery unless it is genuinely appropriate.

Each image needs:
- editorial crop appropriate to the page
- descriptive alt text when informative
- caption when context adds value
- credit
- source/license record in the content system

## Engagement features

High-value stories should use the minimum set of features that materially improves reading:

- pull quote
- highlighted data point
- related-story rail
- author bio
- source list
- simple visual explainer
- captioned image
- newsletter CTA
- next-story recommendation

Do not add widgets merely to increase visual complexity.

## Editorial-team features and held content

Staffroom Review will support a distinct editorial status:

hold

A held story:
- exists in the editorial content system
- may have a title, dek, format, author and notes
- is not linked in public navigation
- is not included in public sitemap/indexing
- can later be promoted to scheduled/published status

Recommended editorial status model:

idea → pitched → commissioned → drafting → editing → approved → scheduled → published → updated → archived

hold can sit alongside this as a controlled publication state.

## Content expansion workflow

The current homepage placeholders are not the final article copy.

### Stage A — Now: content system

Create and maintain:
- format taxonomy
- headline/dek rules
- editorial length bands
- author model
- SEO metadata model
- newsletter model
- blog model
- held-editorial workflow

### Stage B — Before each navbar-page visual approval

For the page currently being built:
- replace short placeholder titles with production-quality headlines
- write production-quality deks
- assign a real format label
- assign an author/byline state
- ensure the visible story cards use realistic line lengths
- populate representative metadata

This must happen before that page's visual freeze, because real editorial copy affects hierarchy and wrapping.

### Stage C — Article-body expansion

After a page family is structurally approved:
- expand its priority stories to full article bodies using the format-specific length bands
- add sourcing, internal links, author metadata and structured data
- complete image credits/captions
- run editorial and SEO QA

### Stage D — Publication pass

Before public launch of a story:
- final copy edit
- fact/source check where relevant
- accessibility check
- metadata check
- image rights check
- canonical/indexing check
- related-link check

## The placeholder principle

Placeholder stories must be credible editorial propositions, not filler.

A placeholder headline should be strong enough to become a real story.
A placeholder dek should be strong enough to become the article's standfirst.
A placeholder format should clearly signal how the piece would be developed.
A future editor should be able to take the record and turn it into a publishable story without inventing an entirely new concept.

## Story registry, intake and verification

All story formats in this document feed a single canonical story registry. Each underlying story receives one permanent Story ID and one master record, regardless of whether it is AI-written, human-written or collaborative.

Before a story is uploaded or published, Staffroom Review must run the Story Intake and Verification process defined in `docs/story-management-system.md`. The check retrieves related stories and reviews:

- duplicate or near-duplicate concepts
- substantial thematic or narrative overlap
- repeated examples or central arguments
- placement/format mismatch
- possible contradiction with earlier Staffroom Review claims or facts

The automated utility is a retrieval/flagging aid rather than a publishing authority. A positive match means the candidate should be compared; it does not by itself prohibit publication. The editor may override the result and the override must be recorded.

The registry also preserves author type and AI involvement so future verification can distinguish Staffroom-generated work from human-contributed work without treating provenance as a quality judgement.


## Article-body production and authorship gate

The article-body stage begins only after the shared article-page system is visually approved.

### Page-by-page rule

Full-length content is completed in this order:

1. Homepage story set.
2. Stories.
3. Teachers.
4. Classrooms.
5. Schools.
6. Ideas.
7. World.
8. Voices.
9. Specialist content products and any remaining dedicated destinations.

Do not start the next page's full-length content while the current page still contains unfinished priority stories.

### Authorship

Every story must have an explicit production path recorded in the Story Registry.

- **Human/editorial-designated:** the manuscript is supplied by the human editor/contributor. The AI workflow does not invent the article body.
- **Staffroom/AI-designated:** the article can be drafted within the documented format and length band, followed by verification and editorial QA.
- **Collaborative:** the supplied human material and AI-assisted drafting/editing are recorded accurately.

“Editorial” is an authorship/production instruction, not a quality rating.

### Article readiness

A story is ready for publication only after:
- full article body is complete;
- Story Intake and Verification has been run;
- author/provenance metadata is accurate;
- sourcing/attribution is complete where relevant;
- image/caption/credit information is complete;
- internal links are present where relevant;
- SEO metadata and canonical URL are checked;
- accessibility and final copy QA are complete.

The shared article route is deliberately gated so placeholder and held stories cannot become public articles before these conditions are met.


## Authorship and AI-use clarification — October 3, 2026

The visible byline is the production gate for article-body authorship:
- **Staffroom Review** byline or **no visible byline**: the piece may be written by the AI-assisted Staffroom workflow, with human/editorial review, verification and publication approval.
- **Editorial** byline on a **Long Read**: the manuscript is human-written and must be supplied by the Editorial Team. AI must not invent, extend or substitute for the manuscript.

This is a provenance rule, not a quality judgement. The publication must accurately preserve the intended authorship state in the registry and article presentation.

### Staged homepage article expansion

Full-length homepage production must be performed **section by section**, not as a single batch. Each section is completed to the applicable format-specific length band, style and article model; then the live site is reviewed before the next section begins. The editor's approval is the gate between sections.

A story is not considered complete merely because it has a plausible body. It must meet the applicable length band, headline/dek rules, opening and body-structure standards, sourcing/attribution requirements, image/credit requirements, SEO metadata, author/byline requirements, accessibility requirements and Story Intake & Verification workflow.
