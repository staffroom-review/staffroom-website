# Staffroom Review — Blog Architecture

## Purpose

The blog is a dedicated lightweight publishing stream for timely, personal and editorially useful writing that does not need the full treatment of a feature.

Primary future URL:

/blog

The blog must feel like Staffroom Review, not like an SEO content farm.

## Role of the blog

Use the blog for:

- short first-person observations
- teaching notes
- editor's notes
- timely reactions
- practical reflections
- reading/resource notes
- behind-the-scenes editorial pieces
- smaller ideas that deserve publication but not a full reported feature

The blog is complementary to the main editorial publication. It should not replace reporting, features, interviews or long-form stories.

## Blog voice

Preferred:
- clear
- personal where appropriate
- specific
- concise
- thoughtful
- useful
- confident without sounding promotional

Avoid:
- generic "10 things teachers should know" repetition
- empty motivational writing
- keyword stuffing
- content assembled purely to attract search traffic
- artificial controversy

## Blog length

Default:
- 700–1,200 words
- 350–700 words for short notes
- 1,200+ only when the piece naturally becomes an essay or feature and should be promoted to another format

These are editorial planning bands, not SEO requirements. Google states there is no preferred word count for ranking. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

## Blog page structure

1. Page H1 and proposition
2. Lead blog post
3. Recent notes grid/list
4. Topic clusters
5. Newsletter cross-promotion
6. Link to main editorial stories

## Blog post template

Every future blog post should support:

- title
- dek
- author
- published date
- updated date when relevant
- reading time
- category
- hero image when useful
- article body
- related stories
- newsletter CTA

## SEO

Every published blog post should have:
- unique title
- H1
- unique meta description
- canonical URL
- descriptive slug
- internal links
- author page
- publication date
- Article/BlogPosting structured data
- social metadata
- sitemap inclusion

Google recommends descriptive titles/headings, crawlable internal links and Article structured data for relevant article pages. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article

## Placeholder blog posts

The placeholder set will be deliberately strong enough to serve as future editorial assignments. Each title should have:
- a clear proposition
- a believable audience
- a defined format
- an obvious angle for expansion
- a useful related-story path

## Relationship to editorial features

A blog post may be promoted into:
- First Person
- Ideas
- Long Read
- Practical Guide

when the editorial scope grows.

That means the content system must store the underlying story independently of its page presentation.

## Navigation

Blog is a specialist destination and should initially sit under the future More navigation rather than displacing the seven primary editorial sections.


## Story registry relationship

Blog posts use the same canonical story registry as the main editorial pages. A blog post receives one Story ID even if it is later promoted into First Person, Ideas, Long Read or another format.

Before a blog post is uploaded or promoted, the Story Intake and Verification process checks the existing registry for repeated concepts, arguments, examples, audience mismatch and possible contradictions.
